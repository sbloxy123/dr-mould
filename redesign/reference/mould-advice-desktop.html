The basics

What causes mould

Mould needs two things: excess moisture, and something organic to feed on. The main causes are:

High humidity

Humid rooms give mould ideal conditions to thrive.

Water leaks

Leaking pipes, roofs or walls bring in the moisture mould needs.

Poor ventilation

Without airflow, moisture can’t escape and builds up.

Condensation

Warm air meeting cold surfaces leaves moisture behind.

Organic materials

Wood, paper, fabric and even dust act as a food source.

Time

In the right conditions mould can start within 24–48 hours and spread quickly.

Deal with the underlying cause and you can prevent, or greatly reduce, mould growth.

Getting help

When to call a professional

Small patches can often be handled yourself. It’s worth getting help if:

  • It’s spread widely. Mould covering a large area or several rooms needs a thorough, organised clean-up.
  • It’s affecting your health. If anyone at home has allergies or breathing problems, safe removal matters.
  • You suspect hidden mould. A musty smell but nothing visible can mean mould behind walls, under floors or in ceilings.
  • It keeps coming back. Recurring mould usually means the root cause hasn’t been found yet.
  • It’s damaged the building. Mould can harm plaster, wood and other materials that may need repair.
  • You’re not sure how to tackle it safely. The right equipment and know-how make a real difference.
  • You’d rather save the time. We’ll handle the whole job quickly and properly.
What we do

About mould removal

We remove harmful mould of all kinds, including black mould (Stachybotrys). It usually comes from leaks, flooding, poor ventilation, weak insulation, high humidity, plumbing problems or poor building techniques.

Mould isn’t always obvious. It can hide behind walls, under floors and in roof spaces, and often only shows once it breaks through the surface or leaves a stain.

Why it matters for your health

Mould exposure is linked to breathing problems, allergies, headaches, tiredness and sore throats. Left alone, it can make rooms unpleasant to live in and more costly to put right.

Prevention

10 ways to reduce mould at home

A family of four can release 7–15 litres of water a day just from cooking, showering and breathing. These habits help keep it in check.

Control humidity

Use a dehumidifier in damp rooms and keep humidity between 30% and 50%.

Ventilate

Run extractor fans while cooking and showering to clear moist air fast.

Fix leaks promptly

Repair leaking pipes, roofs and walls before water can build up.

Insulate properly

Insulated walls, windows and pipes mean less condensation.

Clean regularly

Keep bathrooms and kitchens clean and dry so spores can’t settle.

Mind your houseplants

Overwatering plants adds more moisture to the air than you’d think.

Check lofts & basements

Make sure they’re ventilated and leak-free, and check them now and then.

Dry washing outside

Where you can’t, use a vented dryer or dehumidifier and open a window.

Maintain seals

Check seals around doors and windows to keep moisture out.

Leave a gap

In winter, pull furniture slightly off outside walls so air can circulate.
